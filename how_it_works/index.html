1Four files, four jobs

Your game is made of four files. Each one has its own job.

🧠

The brain

rules.py
  • Knows the map.
  • Knows your health, food and diamonds.
  • Decides what monsters do and who wins a fight.
  • Knows nothing about colors or pictures!
⛏️

The cave brain

caves.py
  • Knows the caves under every Mine.
  • Knows how fast you run and how high you jump.
  • Decides what cave monsters do.
  • Also knows nothing about pictures!
👀👂

The eyes and ears

game.py
  • Listens to your keyboard.
  • Paints everything on the screen.
  • Plays the sounds, the shakes and the sparkles.
  • Asks the brain what to do.
🎨

The art box

sprites.py
  • Holds every picture: the Hero, the monsters, trees, the house and the icons.
  • Each picture is drawn with letters.
🎲 It's like a board game. The rule book says what happens (rules.py, and caves.py for the caves). The board and the pieces are what you see and touch (game.py). The paint set colors the pieces (sprites.py). You can repaint a piece and the rules stay the same.

Why keep them apart? So you can change one without breaking the others. The brain file says so itself, right at the top:

How do they talk? The eyes-and-ears file borrows the other three. In Python that word is import.

What you see in the game

Find the H in the top-left corner. Now look at the picture. There's the house! 🏠 The Ws are the blue lakes and rivers. Can you find the second H, near the bottom? That's another house where you can rest. And the P, the grey Portal stone, is on an island in the big lake. You'll need a boat to get there!

When game.py paints the map, it looks at each letter. G? Just grass. F? Paint a tree. H? Paint the house. M? Paint a mine.

The brain checks the letter too. If the next square is W, you can't go there... unless you've built a boat!

🎥 The camera

The world is too big to fit in the window! So the game uses a camera. It shows 18 squares across and 11 down, and it follows the Hero around to keep him in the middle. Near the edge of the world it stops, so you never see past the edge.

max(0, x) stops the camera at the left and top edges, and min(..., map_w - VIEW_W) stops it at the right edge. (map_w is how wide the world is - Nightfall Hollow is smaller than home!)

To see where you've been, look at the MAP next to the messages at the bottom of the screen. Each tiny square is one square of the world, the blinking yellow square is the Hero, and the white box shows what the camera can see right now.

4The game loop: a super-fast flip-book

The game draws a brand-new picture 60 times every second.

📒 It's like a flip-book. Each page is a little different. Flip the pages fast and the drawing looks like it moves. Your game flips 60 pages every second!

5What happens when you press an arrow key?

One key press starts a whole chain of things, one after another.

🎲 It's like taking a turn in a board game. You move your piece. Then everyone else takes their turn. Then you check what's on your square.

First, the ears (game.py) hear the key. They turn it into a word like "right" and tell the brain: game.do("right").

After you move, the brain runs the rest of the turn:

  1. You move. The Hero steps one square. The fog lifts around him.
  2. Maybe something random happens! In the forest there's a 15% chance you trip and get hurt, and a 12% chance something nice happens. Here a butterfly healed the Hero.
  3. Monsters move. Each monster rolls to see if it moves. If you're 3 squares away or closer, it comes after you! (But never into your house.) This Ember Skull has drifted closer to the Hero.
  4. Skeletons shoot. A Skeleton 1 or 2 squares away has a 35% chance to shoot an arrow. See the white arrow flying at the Hero?
  5. Is a monster on your square? If yes, and you're not home: FIGHT!

7If… then! And rolling dice

An if asks a yes-or-no question. If the answer is yes, the game does something.

🚦 It's like a rule in a game: "If you land on a ladder, then climb up." Your game has lots of rules like that.

Rule: if your health is 0, game over.

First the brain asks: is the Hero still alive? He is alive if his health is more than 0.

When the Hero gets hurt, the brain checks. If he is not alive any more, that's the end — unless he's inside the Shadow King's castle, where he wakes up back at the gate and gets another try:

🎲 Rolling dice

Games need surprises. Your game rolls dice with self.rng.random(). It picks a random number between 0 and 1, like 0.12 or 0.87. Think of it like a giant die with 100 sides: 0.35 is the same as 35 out of 100.

When you search the forest for food:

BERRY_CHANCE is 0.75, so the game asks "is the number smaller than 0.75?" That happens 75 times out of 100. So you find berries 3 times out of 4. (If you picked the Forager power when you leveled up, it uses FORAGER_BERRY_CHANCE instead: 0.9, which is 9 times out of 10.)

🍓 berries 75%
nothing 25%

The else part is for the other 25 times out of 100: you looked, but didn't find anything.

For grown-ups: rng stands for "random number generator". If you start the game with pixi run game --seed 5, the dice roll the same way every time, so the monsters start in the same places. These screenshots used seed 5.

🏠 If you have enough wood…

Press T in the forest to chop wood, and C to craft. Every tree has 8 wood in it. Chop it all, and the tree falls down and leaves an open field! Before it builds anything, the brain asks two questions. Are you on a Forest or Field square? Do you have enough wood? If the answer to either one is no, it tells you why:

!= means "is not", and < means "is smaller than". HOUSE_COST is 5, so with 4 wood you have to chop some more.

8Fights, the Ember Skull fuse, and the fog

A fight is taking turns. You do one thing, then the monster does one thing.

These fights happen on the world map. Down in the caves you fight a different way: see part 9!

Each round, you pick Attack, Run away or Eat. Then it's the monster's turn.

When you attack, the brain rolls the dice. If the number is 0.7 or more, you miss. So you hit 7 times out of 10. Zombies are tough, so your hits on them do 1 less damage.

💥 The Ember Skull's fuse

An Ember Skull has a fuse that counts down: 3, 2, 1… BOOM! After each of your turns, the fuse goes down by 1. You get 3 turns to beat it, or run away. In these screenshots the Hero has no sword, so he can only punch for 1.

⏳ It's like a sand timer in a board game. When the sand runs out, your time is up!

A fight with an Ember Skull starts with the fuse at 3. Other monsters get no fuse (None).

On the monster's turn: take 1 off the fuse. If it's down to 0… BOOM!

🌊 Sea Serpents: watch your boat!

Sea Serpents live in the big lakes and only come after you when you're out in your boat. Their trick is a dice roll. Every round of the fight there's a small chance, 8 out of 100, that it smashes your boat! Then you sink, get hurt, lose your boat, and wash up on the shore. The longer the fight, the more chances it gets, so beat it fast (or row away).

🏹 Your own bow

Skeletons aren't the only ones who can shoot! Make a bow (3 wood) and a bundle of 5 arrows (2 wood) with C (the Craft menu). Then press F, and an arrow flies the way you last walked. Here's how the brain moves it, one square at a time, up to 3 squares. As soon as it finds a monster, it stops (that's what break means):

In a cave, F shoots an arrow straight across the screen the way you're facing.

🌫️ Fog: you only see what's close

The world starts dark. You can only see squares close to you, about 2 squares in every direction, like a circle. Squares you've already visited stay on the map, a little darker.

dr is how many rows away a square is, and dc is how many columns. The math checks if the square is inside a circle around the Hero. Monsters outside the circle are hidden too!

9Caves: running and jumping

Press SPACE on a Mine and you climb down into a cave. The game turns sideways, and now you run and jump!

🛝 It's like a playground. Climb the ledges, jump the gaps, dig for treasure, and don't touch the lava!

On the world map, the game waits for you: one key press, one turn. In a cave it doesn't wait. Everything moves a tiny bit on every page of the flip-book, 60 times a second, even when you're not pressing anything.

What you see

🚀 Speed boxes and gravity

In a cave, the Hero has two speed boxes. vx is how fast he moves left or right, and vy is how fast he moves up or down. Every page, the game moves him by his speed. Then gravity adds a little bit to vy. That's why falling gets faster and faster.

When you press SPACE, his vy is set to minus 14 (a little more for every Springy power you pick). Minus means up! (In computer pictures, the numbers get bigger as you go down the screen.)

Then gravity pulls on him, every single page:

🧱 Bumping into walls

Each page, the Hero moves a tiny step. Then the brain checks: did that step put him inside rock? If it did, the brain pushes him back to the edge of the rock and sets his speed to 0. Bump!

Tricks that make jumping feel good. Lots of real platform games use these:

  • Short hop: let go of SPACE early and gravity gets stronger, so you can do little jumps.
  • Coyote time: you can still jump for a moment after you run off a ledge.
  • Jump buffer: press SPACE just before you land, and the jump still counts.

🍄 Tall caves, four kinds of caves

Tall caves are 20 squares tall, with ledges up to 5 levels high. Land on a bouncy mushroom (o) and BOING, it throws you about 6 blocks up! Grab the glittering gems ($) for a little XP.

Every cave is one of four kinds: stone, crystal, lava or moss. A lava cave glows red, a crystal cave is icy blue, and a moss cave is green and grassy. When you draw a cave, start it with a line like theme: lava to choose its kind.

⛏️ Press R: dig, open, fight

R does whatever makes sense: dig the rock in front of you, open a chest, or swing your sword. F shoots an arrow, and C opens the Craft menu: arrows, a new sword or a pickaxe, right there in the cave. Hold down and press R to dig the floor under your feet. Digging uses the same trick as the map: the brain just changes a letter! A B or a * turns into a ., and the rock is gone.

🕷️ Jump on their heads!

If you're falling (vy > 0) and your feet are near the top of a monster: STOMP! You bounce up, and the monster gets hurt. Touch a monster any other way and it hurts you. Press DOWN in the air for a ground pound: you slam down, and if you land on a monster it hurts it much more (POUND_DAMAGE) and makes it dizzy.

Watch out for spikes and lava, Skeleton arrows, crumbly ledges that fall, and Ember Skulls that go BOOM!

Every Mine has its own cave! All 12 are drawn by hand, one file each in the levels/caves folder. The Mine nearest home plays cave 01, the next one cave 02, and so on: the far Mines have the hardest caves. The game never makes up a cave, so you can open every one of them in the cave editor and change it!

10Try it yourself!

You can change your game! Open a file, change a letter or a number, save, and play.

How to run your game

Open the Terminal and type:

cd ~/adventure-world && pixi run game
Grown-up tips:
  • Make a backup first, so you can always go back: cp -r ~/adventure-world ~/adventure-world-backup
  • No pixi yet? Install it with brew install pixi. Or run the game with python3 game.py after python3 -m pip install pygame.
  • pixi run guide opens this guide.
  • If something goes wrong, Python prints an error. The last lines name the file and line number. Undo your change and try again!
  • The line numbers below are for the files as they are now. Adding lines (like in Challenge 4) moves the lines below them down.
